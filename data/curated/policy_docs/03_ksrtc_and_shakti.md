# KSRTC Travel, the Kanakapura Road Corridor, and the Shakti Scheme

## Why KSRTC matters for a Harohalli commute
Harohalli sits about 35-45 km south of central Bengaluru on the Kanakapura Road
corridor, outside the core BBMP area. BMTC does run services this far out: route
213-V (Harohalli to Sanjay Gandhi Hospital), 213-VD (Dayananda Sagara MDC to
Banashankari / Anjanapura) and 213-VB (Bannikuppe Circle / Harohalli Industrial
Area) all reach Harohalli in the GTFS feed. But the corridor is also served by
KSRTC ordinary buses running between Bengaluru (Kempegowda Bus Station) and
Kanakapura, and those buses continue south to Malavalli and Mysuru.

## KSRTC pass rules
KSRTC issues monthly season tickets between two named places on a daily-commute
basis. The rules that matter:

- Monthly passes are issued for travel along with a KSRTC photo-identity card,
  which costs Rs 50 and is valid for one year.
- A pass issued for Express services is valid on both Express and Ordinary
  services. A pass issued for Ordinary services is valid only on Ordinary services.
- Season tickets are personal and not transferable.
- Student concessional passes are the cheapest option where they apply, and since
  2026-27 they are free for non-AC services under the state scheme.

KSRTC does not publish a single statewide fare table for season tickets. Each
depot prices a pass by the corridor and the number of fare stages. The agent must
never invent a KSRTC monthly pass price; it should quote the corridor and send the
student to the depot.

## Published KSRTC fares on the corridor
Karnataka raised all RTC fares by 15 per cent with effect from 5 January 2025. The
revised ordinary fare between Bengaluru (KBS) and Kanakapura is Rs 124, up from
Rs 107. The equivalent fare to Mysuru is Rs 162.

There is no published fare for the Harohalli fare stage in any source we could
reach. Scaling the published KBS-Kanakapura fare by distance gives roughly Rs 86
one way to Harohalli, but that is an estimate, not a tariff. The honest answer is:
budget about Rs 80-90 and confirm at the depot or on the KSRTC app.

## Shakti Scheme — free travel for women
The Shakti scheme has provided free travel for women and transgender residents of
Karnataka on non-premium state buses since 11 June 2023. Key rules:

- Valid on BMTC ordinary and metro feeder services and on KSRTC ordinary and
  express services within Karnataka.
- Not valid on AC services, Vajra, Vayu Vajra, Airavat, Ambari, Rajahamsa or other
  luxury classes.
- Not valid for the out-of-state portion of an interstate journey. A woman on an
  Udupi-Kasaragod service still pays for the Kerala leg.
- Requires proof of Karnataka residence, usually Aadhaar. A Shakti smart card is
  available through Seva Sindhu but is not mandatory for most travel.

For a woman Karnataka resident who does not need AC, Shakti makes every paid
non-AC pass redundant. It therefore dominates any cost optimisation: the correct
recommendation is to carry ID and buy nothing. Girl students do not need a separate
student pass for the same reason.

## Choosing between BMTC, KSRTC and the metro on this corridor
- If both ends are inside the BMTC network, use BMTC. Fares are lower and the
  ordinary monthly pass or free student pass applies.
- If one end is beyond the BMTC network, a KSRTC ordinary leg is usually needed.
  A KSRTC student pass or the free student pass covers it.
- The metro reaches Silk Institute on the Green Line, which is the closest metro
  station to the Kanakapura Road corridor. From Silk Institute onward you still
  need a bus, so a metro-plus-bus commute is only competitive for trips that start
  on the Green Line.
